Conceptual Design

The capstone: size a whole aircraft from a mission, the way conceptual design really works — then take the result into the other tools.

The loop
  1. Mission — what must it do?
  2. Weight sizing — fuel + empty-weight fractions converge the take-off weight
  3. Wing sizing — wing loading sets area, span, chords
  4. Closure — does it actually meet the mission?
Tutorial → project

Load the worked example to watch a light trainer size itself, then change the numbers to design your own aircraft.

Then go deeper

Take the sized wing to Wing / VLM for its lift + stability, and pick its section in Airfoil Lab (aim for the cruise CL).

Method

Raymer fuel-fraction sizing, the Breguet range equation, an empty-weight regression, and ISA — first-order estimates, pure Python.

3 · Weight sizing
MTOW is a fixed point — a heavier aircraft needs more fuel and structure, which makes it heavier. The loop iterates until it settles.
4 · Wing
The stall requirement caps wing loading (W/S); with the MTOW that fixes the wing area, and AR + taper give span and chords.
5 · Closure
Does it meet the mission? Check the stall speed, the cruise lift coefficient, and the power/thrust required.
